# Kannada content and editorial method

Research date: 11 October 2026. Version one contains 60 short phrases across six everyday situations and a 0–9 pronunciation reference.

The situations, English prompts, hints, assembly chunks, and lesson ordering are original. The Kannada consists of common short expressions and newly composed examples using basic vocabulary and polite verb forms. We did not copy another course's dialogue, exercise sequence, prose, or audio. Names, routes, transactions, and the OTP 4826 are fictional learning examples.

## Coverage and editorial decisions

- **Auto:** destination, ride code, left, right, straight, slower travel, waiting, stopping, fare, and UPI. The code is spoken digit by digit. `anna` literally means elder brother and is a familiar respectful address for a man; learners may omit it. It is not a universal title for every driver.
- **Breakfast:** quantities, idli, dosa, coffee, water, sugar, spice, availability, takeaway, bill, and thanks. The coffee hint also teaches the substitution for tea.
- **Groceries:** stock, prices, half/full kilo, alternatives, a reusable bag, total, payment, change, and receipt.
- **Transit:** a bus route, platform, ticket, fare, departure, next stop, getting down, help, metro location, and seats.
- **Delivery:** location, a shop landmark, gate, floor, arrival, waiting, coming down, handover, payment, and thanks.
- **Neighbours:** greetings, fictional-name introduction, being new, learning Kannada, repetition, slower speech, not understanding, help, farewell, and wellbeing.

The OTP, UPI, delivery, floor, stock, receipt, and several neighbour examples are **composed examples**, not quotations from an attested phrase list. They use restrained everyday vocabulary. They need the same editorial review as any newly written lesson.

Kannada script is the audio input and canonical text. Roman text is a beginner aid, not formal transliteration or IPA: `aa`, `ee`, and `oo` usually signal long vowels; Kannada retroflex consonants and vowel length cannot be represented reliably by familiar English spelling alone. The app teaches one useful form, while hints mention a few common contractions. English-derived words such as OTP, UPI, bus, ticket, bill, metro, gate, and parcel are identified where helpful. Listening is more useful than reading Roman text as if it were English.

## Limits and corrections

These materials have been researched and edited with AI assistance. They have **not been reviewed or recorded by a native Kannada teacher**. Do not describe them as certified translations, comprehensive pronunciation training, or natural-speech recordings. Variants differ across speakers and parts of Karnataka; a valid alternative need not be an error.

Audio is synthetic Kannada speech generated for this project, not copied from the cited sources. Synthetic speech may mispronounce loanwords, proper nouns, abbreviations, or expressive speech. The game is an introduction to practical phrases and does not grade the learner's accent or speech.

For a correction, report the stable phrase ID, the existing text, a proposed Kannada replacement, the intended meaning, and (if available) a reliable reference or explanation of local usage. Update script, Roman text, assembly chunks, hints, and both audio speeds together. Re-run content validation after changes. Native-speaker review is a valuable next quality improvement, especially for composed examples and loanwords.
